About — Est. 2018

Made to be used, not preserved.

Grayfield started as a two-person operation making canvas work coats for a hardware co-op in Portland. We've grown, but the standard hasn't changed: if it won't hold up to a full day of actual work, it doesn't go on the rack.

Where it started

A canvas coat that wouldn't quit.

In 2018, our founder was patching the same waxed canvas coat for the third winter in a row and realized the mass-market alternatives weren't built to last a season, let alone a decade. Grayfield's first run was 40 work coats, cut and sewn in a rented corner of a Portland upholstery shop, sold directly to people who wore them to work every day.

That first batch is still the standard we cut against. Every piece we've added since — flannel, denim, wool — had to prove it belonged next to that coat before it made the collection.

Materials

What we build with, and why

Four materials make up almost everything we sell. We stock them deep instead of chasing new fabrics every season.

01 — Canvas

12oz cotton duck

Woven in North Carolina, waxed in small batches. Softens and darkens at the wear points over years, not weeks.

02 — Denim

13oz selvedge

Shuttle-loomed on vintage frames, rigid off the bolt so it breaks in around you, not the mannequin.

03 — Wool

Merino knit

Non-itch, temperature-regulating, and one of the only fibers in our lineup that's fully biodegradable.

04 — Flannel

Brushed cotton twill

Double-napped for warmth without bulk, woven in a small check that hides a long day's wear.

Process

From mill to your closet

  1. 01

    Sourcing

    We work with the same four mills we started with — long relationships mean better fabric, not just lower prices.

  2. 02

    Pattern & cut

    Every pattern is tested by our own crew doing the actual work the garment is built for, then adjusted before it's approved.

  3. 03

    Small-batch sewing

    We run small production batches so a flaw gets caught by a person, not buried in a container of ten thousand units.
